# Dyev website direction

## Visitor journey

1. Understand Dyev in one sentence.
2. Find a product that can be used now.
3. See what is still being researched in Dyev Labs.
4. See the AI work and its current stage.
5. Contact Dyev with a question or an idea.

The main navigation follows that journey: Products & Solutions, Dyev Labs, AI, About.

## Content rules

- Use short, ordinary sentences. Explain what a project does before describing its method.
- Show a status for named work. "Available now" links to a working product; "In development" does not imply a release date.
- Avoid claims about features or results until they are verified.
- Keep research separate from released products.
- Add future projects to the section that matches their stage.

## Visual system

- Keep the existing off-white, charcoal, and cobalt-blue palette.
- Use one main headline and one supporting paragraph at each page entry. Avoid small labels above headings.
- Body text is 17–21px in the main presentation. Small text is reserved for navigation and short labels.
- Build page rhythm with open space, rules, bands, and diagrams. Product rows carry the work without repeating cards.

## Motion

- Entrance: a short upward fade for each major section as it enters view.
- Continuity: a thin progress line at the top of the viewport.
- Identity: slow motion in the hero mark and the Labs and AI diagrams.
- Interaction: small movement on links and buttons.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; all content remains available without animation or JavaScript.

## Follow-up content

Tutor Board is described as in development and product validation. Sentinel is described as a concept in early development. Their project pages distinguish planned capabilities from shipped features. Add verified screenshots, repositories, and working links only when those projects are ready to share.
